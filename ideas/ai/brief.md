Give exactly **10** ideas. What makes an idea great for this format:
- One surprising experiment with a physical cause and effect you can SHOW ("what happens if…").
- Stakes that escalate in three steps, and a twist in the last line (ironic, set up earlier).
- Can be drawn as flat 2D on black (Flow) or built from simple 3D lab props (code engine): glasses, balls, scales,

- Mix across physics, chemistry, the human body, everyday objects, extreme numbers, myths.
- Vary the narrative shape across the 10 ideas (at least 5 different shapes) and name it in `narrative`:
  - `classic` — Experiment arc: the hook states the paradox → setup shows the normal case → the experiment starts → two or three escalating stages, each stranger than the last → payoff (the measured result) → twist (one ironic line set up earlier).
  - `countdown` — Countdown: the hook promises four or five surprising effects and teases number one without revealing it. Then count DOWN: each item is one stage ('Number five: …') with one fact and one consequence, each stronger than the last. Number one is the payoff; the twist follows it.
  - `timeline` — Clock: walk through time in fixed jumps (seconds, minutes, hours, days or years — whatever fits the topic). Each stage opens on the time ('Minute ten.', 'Hour six.') and says what has changed by then. The jumps may grow (one minute → ten minutes → one hour → one day). The last stop is the payoff; the twist reframes the whole timeline.
  - `myth_fact` — Myth vs fact: the hook states the belief everybody holds, as if it were true. Then bust it in rounds: two or three myths, each = the myth in one line → the test or evidence → the fact with one number. The payoff is what really happens; the twist is the one part of the myth that was secretly right (or why the myth exists at all).
  - `escalation` — Times ten: run the same experiment at one, then ten, a hundred, a thousand… (or divide by ten going small). Each step is one stage: the new amount → what changes → one number. Keep going until something absurd or a hard limit — the breaking point is the payoff. The twist brings it back to human scale.
  - `journey` — Journey: follow ONE thing from start to end — a sip of water through the body, a photon from the sun, a coin through the economy. Narrate its route stop by stop ('First it hits… then… by the time it reaches…'). Each stop is one stage with one fact. The destination is the payoff; the twist is where it really ends up or how long it really took.
  - `versus` — Versus: two contenders (two objects, two strategies, two animals…). Introduce both in the hook. Then two or three rounds, each one measurable test with a winner and a running score. An upset in the last round; the payoff names the winner; the twist says why the loser still matters (or the hidden cost of winning).
  - `rewind` — Rewind: open on the final outcome — the most extreme moment — as the hook. Then rewind to zero ('Let's go back.') and walk forward fast through three or four stages that explain how it got there. The payoff catches up with the opening image; the twist reveals the detail the opening hid.
  - `chain_reaction` — Chain reaction: one small trigger → domino one → domino two → domino three, each caused by the last ('…which means… which means…'). The tipping point is the payoff; the twist is how small the trigger was.
  - `mystery` — Mystery: open with a strange documented fact that seems impossible. Offer the intuitive guess and kill it. Zoom into the real cause clue by clue (clue → clue → mechanism). The payoff is the mechanism; the twist adds the one detail that makes it even stranger.
